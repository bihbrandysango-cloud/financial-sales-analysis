# Financial sales analysis: speech

About 8 minutes of talking at 140 words a minute, plus pauses to point at charts and a short question break. That fills a 10-minute slot. Each section names its slide. Every figure was checked against the Power BI model.

## Slide 1: Title (about 15 seconds)

Thank you for having me. I'm Brandy Sango. I'm going to walk you through a financial sales analysis: 700 transactions, five countries, and one question. Where does the money stay?

## Slide 2: The problem (about 50 seconds)

The business sold $118.7 million over sixteen months. But only 14 cents of every dollar became profit, $16.9 million in all.

Leaders need to know three things. Which products, markets and customer segments earn that profit. Where the business loses money, and why. And when sales peak, so they can stock up and plan around it. Everything in this talk answers one of those three.

## Slide 3: The data (about 40 seconds)

The data is one table of 700 sales transactions, from September 2013 to December 2014. It covers five countries, six products and five customer segments. Every figure is in US dollars.

One warning before the charts. 2013 has only four months, September to December, while 2014 has twelve. So I never compare the two years as wholes. Where months are compared, I say which year they come from.

## Slide 4: Overview (about 50 seconds)

This is the Executive Overview page of the report. Sales are $118.7 million and profit is $16.9 million, a margin of 14.2 percent. The business moved 1.13 million units.

The five country tiles along the top show how even the markets are. The United States leads with $25.0 million and Mexico is last with $20.9 million. That is a spread of about 20 percent. Note that the market with the most sales is not the one with the best margin. Keep that in mind for the next slide.

## Slide 5: Countries (about 55 seconds)

Five markets, close in size. The United States sold $25.0 million, Canada $24.9 million, France $24.4 million, Germany $23.5 million and Mexico $20.9 million.

Profit tells a different story. Germany earns the best margin at 15.7 percent and France follows at 15.5. The United States, our biggest market, earns the lowest, 12.0 percent. It sells the most and keeps the least of each dollar.

## Slide 6: Products (about 50 seconds)

Paseo is the product that carries the business. It earned $4.8 million in profit, 28 percent of the total, and sold 338,000 units, twice as many as the next product.

Amarilla has the best margin at 15.9 percent. Velo has the worst at 12.6 percent. There are six products and the gap between best and worst margin is only about three points, so product choice matters less than segment and discount.

## Slide 7: Segments (about 60 seconds)

This is where the money is made, and where it leaks.

Government earns $11.4 million in profit. That is 67 percent of everything the business keeps. Small Business adds $4.1 million.

Enterprise loses money. It sold $19.6 million and lost $0.6 million, a margin of minus 3.1 percent. That is one dollar in six of all sales, and it brings in no profit at all.

Channel Partners go the other way. They earn a 73 percent margin, but they only sold $1.8 million.

## Slide 8: Discounts (about 60 seconds)

Discounts took $9.2 million, or 7.2 percent of gross sales. The deeper the discount, the thinner the margin.

With no discount, margin is 21.9 percent. At a low discount it is 17.9, at medium 14.4, and at high just 9.1 percent.

And the loss-making sales sit exactly here. There are 58 sales that lost money. Every one is in the Enterprise segment, and every one carried a medium or high discount: 33 high and 25 medium. So the Enterprise problem is a discount problem.

## Slide 9: Months (about 50 seconds)

December 2014 was the biggest month for profit at $2.0 million, and October 2014 was the biggest for sales at $12.4 million. Both months had 70 transactions, double the usual 35.

October 2013 also ran at 70 transactions, though December 2013 did not, so the peaks are not perfectly regular. Paseo is the top product in the busiest month, December 2014, at $4.7 million.

The caution again: 2013 covers only September to December, so I can't call this a yearly pattern. It is what sixteen months show.

## Slide 10: Recommendations (about 70 seconds)

Five things to do, in order.

One: protect Government. It earns two thirds of the profit, so keep pricing and service strong there.

Two: fix Enterprise. Review those 58 loss-making sales and stop the medium and high discounts that push them below cost.

Three: cap deep discounts. Margin falls to 9.1 percent at the high band. Set a limit and require approval for exceptions.

Four: back Paseo and stock for the fourth quarter. It leads profit and units, and October and December are the biggest months.

Five: grow Mexico and Channel Partners. Mexico sells the least, and Channel Partners earn a 73 percent margin on only $1.8 million of sales.

I put them in this order because the first two protect or recover the most profit, and the last is the easiest to start.

## Slide 11: Conclusion (about 45 seconds)

The business is healthy but it leaks margin through one segment and one habit. Government carries the profit. Enterprise loses money, and deep discounts are how it loses it. Fix Enterprise and the discounts first. Neither needs new customers.

Two limits to say out loud. The data covers 16 months, so I would confirm these trends with a longer history before committing budget. And the data shows what happened, not why, so the first step of any change is to ask the sales team why those 58 discounts were given.

Thank you. I'm happy to take questions.

## Likely questions

**Which product earned the most profit?** Paseo, at $4.8 million.

**Which country sold the most?** The United States, at $25.0 million. It also shows highest on the map.

**Which segment earned the least?** Enterprise, which lost $0.6 million.

**How many records belong to 2014?** 525 of the 700.

**Which product sold the most units?** Paseo again, 338,000.

**Which month and product combination had the highest sales?** Paseo in December 2014, at $4.7 million.

**What is sold in Mexico?** All six products. Together they earned $2.9 million in profit, led by Paseo at $0.9 million. Velo earned the least there, $0.2 million.

**Why is the loss only in Enterprise?** Every one of the 58 loss-making sales is Enterprise with a medium or high discount. I can't tell from the data why those discounts were approved.

**Is 16 months enough?** It is enough to see where profit and loss sit, but not enough to call a seasonal pattern. I would want at least two full years.

**What would you do first?** Review the Enterprise discounts. That is the one change that turns a loss into a profit without finding a new customer.
